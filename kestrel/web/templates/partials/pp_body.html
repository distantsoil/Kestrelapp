The short version

Kestrel builds one pack per interview: the likely questions, which of your stories fits each, the gaps to handle, and questions to ask.

What you do

  • Fix questions marked No story yet.
  • Tick the questions you want to ask.
  • Practice, then download the PDF.

What it never does

It never contacts the company or anyone on the panel, and never rewrites your stories.

{% endcall %} {%- if tab == 'pack' %} {% call ui.flyout('pp-pk1-fly', 'How the pack is built', help_href='/help') %}

What goes in

The posting, the gaps Kestrel found when it rated the role, and public information about the company. Nothing from other applications.

How a question gets a story

Kestrel compares each likely question with your saved stories and picks the closest one. It never writes a story for you. If nothing fits, the question says No story yet.

How to use it

  • Fix the questions marked No story yet first.
  • Read the pack the morning of the interview.
  • Tick the questions you want in the PDF.
{% endcall %} {% call ui.flyout('pp-pk2-fly', 'Your words only', help_href='/help') %}

Why this matters

Interviewers can tell when an answer is not yours. Every story in the pack is the text you saved, character for character.

If you swap a story

Pick another one from your bank or write a new one. The choice is for that question on this role, and a rebuilt pack keeps it.

If a number is wrong

Edit the story. Your version replaces the old one everywhere it is used.

{% endcall %} {% call ui.flyout('pp-pk3-fly', 'Questions to ask them', help_href='/help') %}

Why they are specific

Each question comes from something Kestrel could not find out about the company, so it is a real question and not a generic one.

How many

Three or four is plenty. Asking fewer, better questions reads as preparation.

After the date passes, open the role page to note what was asked, who was in the room and what happens next. That capture stays there.

Your notes

What you write in Your notes is saved on this computer. Build stories can read it, and it is never sent anywhere.

The claim check

Kestrel flags anything you say that is not in your resume or stories. If it is true, add it as a story so Kestrel can back it up. If not, leave it out on the day.

How to read the bars

Bars are out of 5. Under 3.5 is worth a second try. They show how this answer scored, not how you compare with other people.

{% endcall %} {% call ui.flyout('pp-pr3-fly', 'Getting better', help_href='/help') %}

Why 5

Fewer sessions say more about the question you drew than about you, so Kestrel says there are too few until there are five.

A good routine

  • Three questions a day for three days.
  • Say the answer out loud, then type it.
  • Redo the one with the lowest bar.

Where stories come from

How a proposal is made

Kestrel compares what each sketch says, not the titles. Two sketches about the same project look alike even when named differently.

Reviewing

You see the proposed story and write the final version yourself. Keep separate removes the proposal and Kestrel does not offer it again.
