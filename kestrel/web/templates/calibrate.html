What you do

  1. Kestrel shows the roles it is least sure about.
  2. You say how good a match each one is.
  3. Then you see Kestrel's score, and it learns from the difference.

Why your score comes first

Seeing Kestrel's score first tends to pull yours toward it. Your own call teaches it more.

What your ratings change

At {{ strips.few_at }} ratings, they become examples Kestrel shows the AI when it scores similar roles. At {{ strips.tune_at }} it offers a scoring tune-up you can accept or skip. At {{ strips.rank_at }}, Kestrel learns to sort new roles your way. It tests itself on your ratings first and is only used if it gets more of them right. After that, keep rating whenever Kestrel asks: your taste changes, and new kinds of roles show up.

Nothing leaves your computer

Your ratings stay in your Kestrel data folder.

{% endcall %} {% call ui.flyout('cal-change-fly', 'What your ratings change', help_href='/help') %}

Right away

Each rating is saved as one of your own examples. From {{ strips.few_at }} ratings, Kestrel shows the AI a few of them when it scores a similar role.

At {{ strips.tune_at }}

Kestrel checks whether different weights on the five scores would match your ratings better. You choose: use the new weights or keep the current ones. Nothing changes without your yes.

At {{ strips.rank_at }}

Kestrel trains a sorter on your ratings and tests it on ratings it has not seen. It is used only if it gets more of them right than the usual scoring. It tries again every 50 ratings.
